# Rules for this repository

**Tests:** `luajit tests/run.lua` from the repository root (LuaJIT at `ProjectObsidian/ObsidianLauncher/target/luajit/luajit.exe`). `tests/test_game.lua` runs the game's own compiled generator (`tests/game.lua` loads the `.ljbc` dumps from `ObsidianLauncher/internal/research/scripts`, never committed here) with the mod hooked in; on GitHub those tests skip. After any change to `src/level.lua` or `src/shapes.lua` also run `luajit tools/soak.lua 600`: it must end with `0 with a problem`.

**How it works:** `src/level.lua` wraps the steps of `GeneratedLevel`. The mod makes the outline (`shapes.lua`), says which biome each cell is (`regions.lua`), works out heights and tilts after placement (`relief.lua`) and plans props (`props.lua`); placing objectives and set pieces and tracing the edge stay the game's functions, called on tables the mod prepared. `profile.lua` turns what a rule answered into settled, clamped choices.

**What must hold:**

- Every machine builds the same map. Nothing in a step may use `math.random`, `pairs` order over edited tables (use `grid.sorted`), time, or anything one machine alone knows. The mod's numbers come from `src/rng.lua`, forked by name per step, and a step draws the same count of numbers whatever it then does with them.
- The mod never draws from the game's `self.random`, so the game's steps take the numbers they always took.
- Shapes stay on the lattice of 2 by 2 cell blocks with no blocks joined by a corner alone: the game's edge tracing was only ever run on such areas.
- Lists the game draws from without looking (`common`, `extract`, the three `edge_*` it needs, `mission`) are never left empty.
- A failure in the mod's own part of a step is logged and marks the level `broken`; the rest of that map is then the game's.
- Without a rule, or with a profile that changes nothing, a map is the game's own, piece for piece (a test compares).

**Game facts this rests on** (build 6001293): the level tile is 24 m; positions travel as 16 bits over -1000 to 1000 m; `level_identifier` is 14 bits; the map texture is 1872 px at 72 px a cell on PC; `GeneratedLevel` steps are called one by one from `generate_all`, the briefing's `MapWidget` and the planet screen.

**Flat ground the game assumes** (found with the in-game probe, 2026-11-14), each handled in `level.lua` by telling the game positions from the terrain's height while it looks:

- `SpawnManager` takes a spawn ray's hit only between -0.5 and 1 m (group spawn points -0.2 to 1 m); without the fix the players never dropped and the mission stayed black.
- `NavMeshMoverComponent.move_to_ground` takes ground only below 2 m and at most 0.75 m above an enemy's feet, and answers 0 otherwise: enemies walked under raised ground.
- The camera calls `get_closest_position_on_level` every frame from a point at z 0 and moves by the difference: never answer it with another height.
- A tilted level covers less ground by the cosine of its tilt, and its walls lean apart from those of a neighbour with another tilt (slits and black wedges, seen in game). Each unit of a tilted level is drawn sheared (`Unit.set_local_pose` with a matrix that is not a turn: the engine draws it) so that the flat piece lies on the slope; the collision keeps the tilted load, with slits of a few centimetres on borders, and ground rays that fall into one are cast again beside it.
- `World.spawn_unit` with a hashed name (`#ID[...]`) crashes the game: props need names, and the units of levels have none.
- A world holds at most 65536 units: past that the game hangs, goes black or crashes when the next unit spawns (the player's gear, 2026-11-23). Pieces hold 25 (forest) to 85 (lava) units on average (`tests/data/units.lua`, probe `unitdump`). `M.OWN_UNITS` caps the planned map, `M.UNIT_BUDGET` stops the backdrop.
- Loading a level with a scale after the rotation draws it scaled, but its collision keeps the size of the level.
- Frame rate (probe `bench.py`, 2026-11-26): the engine draws every unit in view however far or small; frame time follows the units in view, and hidden units cost next to nothing. A Vast map with a long view ran at a third of a standard map's rate. `src/view.lua` hides meshes by distance and size. Use mesh visibility, only on meshes visible at load (`Unit.mesh_visibility`): levels hold hidden units and meshes (volumes drawn as red sheets once shown). Level units can die or be replaced during play: the view touches a unit only while it is alive and has the name and mesh count recorded (defensive; the crashes first blamed on this were the probe's freeze effect, see `internal/in-game-checks.md`). Probe runs use `god`, not `safe`. City pieces keep a cost even when hidden (lights are about a tenth of it; the rest unknown).
- A viewport has one shading environment; props of another biome read values from it that no script can set (city vehicles plain white under a desert environment, fine under the city's). `M.light` switches the viewport to the biome under the camera (`World.set_shading_environment` through the viewport proxy) and eases the colours. Package property preferences: a higher number is preferred (a variant added at 1.5 beat the planet's at 1 and changed the city signs).
- Gaps: a tilted level's collision is shorter across by the cosine of its tilt (1.5 m between two pieces at 20 degrees). `relief.bridges` loads `desert/common_1x1_genericdesert_01` (2 units) hidden under each tilted border, shifted 2 m toward it on the plane of the cell; the player walked up and down 28-degree slopes on them (probe 2026-11-26).
- Engine API tables (`PhysicsWorld`) can have fields replaced; the game's `dofile` reads its bundles, not the disk, and `loadstring` answers nothing. Logging from inside an engine callback (a raycast's) is lost.

**In-game probe** (main PC only, with the user's word; never on the notebook): `internal/probe`. `run.py begin` takes `X:\Projects\Helldivers\Helldivers 1\.game-lock` (shared with other sessions), records the real saves and purges the real library, then deploys Obsidian API, Patches, this mod (packed) and the probe mod into a throwaway data folder; `run.py redeploy [maps key=value ...]` after a change (adds Revamped Maps with options); `run.py end` restores everything and drops the lock. `drive.py <out> launch wait:... cmd:... shot:...` drives the game; `tour.py` plays one practice mission per profile. Probe commands: `profile key=value ...`, `practice time env enemy difficulty [layout]`, `state`, `relief`, `goto_high`, `goto_edge`, `enemies`, `group`, `safe`, `god`, `abort`, `cam up <height> <pitch> <yaw> [back]` and `cam x y z yaw pitch` (Obsidian API free camera), `camoff`, `fog` (off, for shots from above), `bounds`, `grid off=0` (ground rays over the map, misses by distance to a border), `shearcheck`, `biomeground`, `goto_biome`, `shadeenv`, `shadevals`, `view`, `hide`, `cullsim`, `lights`, `unitstats`, `keys`. Eyes of Liberty is deployed too (third person shots; `look:dx,dy` and `hold:KEY:secs` in drive.py turn and walk); switch it off (`obsidian disable alomare.eyesofliberty`, deploy) for shots from above, because it gives the fog back to the planet while the free camera is held. `montage.py` puts shots in one picture. The probe keeps its own save (`wgprobe_<id>`). Relaunch the game every few missions (see the crash note in `internal/in-game-checks.md`).

**Frame rate bench:** `python internal/probe/bench.py <name> "<profile>" "<practice>" [probe command ...]` launches once, puts a free camera at the middle of the map looking four ways with a 1500 m view, and logs the frame rate per view after each command (`view 0.7`, `cullsim ...`); results in `runs/bench/results.txt`.

**Option matrix** (after any change that could reach the game): `python internal/probe/matrix.py [first] [count]` plays one practice mission per row of Revamped Maps options (every size, outline, biome count, layout, relief, backdrop and objective setting, on all five planet types, three factions, difficulty 1 to 12) and checks that it loads, that the map matches the options, the height reached, the backdrop, the units in the world, enemies on the ground and the frame rate; results in `internal/probe/runs/matrix/results.txt`. `sh internal/probe/seedrun.sh <seed> key=value ...` replays a seed from a player's log (`map <seed>:` line) with given options (`DBG=` switches of `M.dbg`, `TRACE=loads` logs every level load). The driver kills a game whose heartbeat (`logs/wgprobe.beat`) stops for 10 s (45 s while loading) or that shows a black screen twice a second apart, 10 s into a mission, so no run waits out a hang.
